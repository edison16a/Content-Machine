# Step 2 (Clip): plan the cuts

Read the whole transcript and find the best moments. You only have the words, so look for what the words reveal: reveals, big reactions, exclamations and laughter cues, funny exchanges, surprising numbers, arguments, emotional beats, shifts in energy, quotable lines, fails and clutch moments. If you're unsure what a moment looks like, extract a still frame to look at (never to produce deliverables).

- Target count = days of clips × 3. Shortlist about twice as many candidates. Each moment must be self-contained: a stranger understands it from the title plus the video.
- Length 10 to 45 seconds (usually 15 to 30). Start at the beginning of a sentence a beat before the action; end right after the payoff. Never more than 59.98s.
- Score each 1 to 10: something happens in the first 3 seconds, stand-alone clarity, payoff, rewatch value. Keep the top moments scoring 6 or higher, then order them **chronologically**. If fewer qualify, make fewer, say so, and don't pad. No more than 3 from any single 5-minute stretch if avoidable.
- **Titles.** Each clip gets its own on-video title that explains what happened in a way that makes someone need to watch. Write a fresh title for every clip from what actually happens in it.
  - Eight words or fewer, about 44 characters at most (it must fit on two lines). No emojis.
  - Third person, specific names and details from the footage; the most important idea first.
  - Rotate between styles (approaches, not text to copy): a curiosity question; a stakes statement; a reaction framing naming who reacts to what; a deadpan understatement against the chaos; a number or fact that sounds impossible; a "how did they / how can anyone" disbelief line; a bold short verdict.
  - No two consecutive titles share a structure or opening word. Never promise something the clip doesn't show.
  - Give each title one **accent** word or phrase: a number, a name or the key noun.
- Write `plan/plan.json` with stable integer ids and a short `note` per item.
